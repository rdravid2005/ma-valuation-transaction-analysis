# Precedent Transactions Analysis

## Purpose

Precedent transactions estimate how strategic buyers have valued comparable acquisition targets. Unlike trading comparables, transaction multiples can incorporate a control premium and expected synergies. The method is therefore useful for framing what an acquirer may need to pay, but it does not independently prove that the purchase price creates value.

Altair is displayed as a relevant industrial-technology transaction but excluded from the core multiple statistics. Its software-led economics and valuation are not sufficiently comparable to Cognex's hardware-led machine-vision profile.

## Methodology and limitations

- Transaction enterprise value is paired with target operating metrics to maintain numerator/denominator consistency.
- The core set includes six machine-vision, sensing, and automation transactions. Altair is treated as an outlier.
- Private-company and carve-out disclosures are incomplete. No undisclosed EBITDA or EBIT figures were invented.
- Moritex revenue is an explicit estimate based on the midpoint of Cognex's announced 6%–8% revenue-contribution guidance. Matrox revenue is the buyer's announced estimate.
- Premium statistics use public targets with disclosed reference prices. Reference periods differ, so the premium output is corroborative rather than directly comparable in every case.
- Only two core transactions provide usable EBITDA and EBIT data. EV / Revenue receives the greatest weight; EV / EBITDA is supportive, and EV / EBIT is corroborative.

## Valuation results

| Method | 25th percentile | Median | 75th percentile | Implied Cognex value per share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| EV / Revenue | 4.4x | 6.0x | 7.1x | $34.06–$52.57 |
| EV / EBITDA | 17.6x | 18.2x | 18.8x | $39.09–$41.46 |
| EV / EBIT | 27.0x | 28.7x | 30.4x | $51.98–$58.12 |
| Premium paid | 34.8% | 41.5% | 44.5% | $80.49–$86.31 |

The core precedent median range is $40.27–$45.32 per Cognex share when EV / Revenue and EV / EBITDA are emphasized. Applying the median disclosed control premium to Cognex's $59.73 unaffected share price produces an $84.52 indication.

## Interpretation

The wide gap between fundamental transaction multiples and the premium-paid method is a central deal issue, not a modeling error. Cognex's current market price already exceeds the standalone DCF and the core public-company and precedent-multiple indications. A credible acquisition proposal would likely need to exceed the unaffected price, which would require Rockwell to justify a materially higher valuation through defensible synergies, strategic scarcity, or stronger operating assumptions.

The premium-paid method estimates the price necessary to obtain control; it does not establish intrinsic value or shareholder value creation. The valuation summary reconciles DCF, trading comparables, precedents, and the unaffected share price before establishing an illustrative offer-price framework.
