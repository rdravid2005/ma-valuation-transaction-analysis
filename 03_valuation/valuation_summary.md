# Valuation Summary and Football Field

## Purpose

The valuation summary reconciles the DCF, trading comparables, precedent transactions, unaffected share price, and control-premium evidence. Investment bankers use this analysis to frame negotiation ranges and advise on price; they do not mechanically average every methodology because each method answers a different question.

The DCF range uses the four sensitivity cases surrounding the base case: approximately ±1.0% WACC and ±0.5% terminal growth. Market- and transaction-multiple ranges use the 25th and 75th percentiles with the median displayed as the midpoint.

## Valuation conclusion

The selected standalone fundamental range is $40–$55 per Cognex share. This range gives greater weight to EV / Revenue and EV / EBITDA while using EV / EBIT and P / E as corroborative methods. The DCF remains an intrinsic-value anchor but receives less weight because its high WACC produces values materially below current market pricing.

The $59.73 unaffected price exceeds the selected standalone range. This indicates that Cognex's market price already reflects expectations beyond the base operating forecast, including stronger future growth, scarcity value, or potential strategic interest.

## Acquisition framework

The illustrative acquisition range is $75–$85 per share, with an $80 midpoint. This is not a weighted average of the standalone methods. It is a negotiation framework anchored by the unaffected market price and the observed precedent-premium range.

At $80 per share, the preliminary transaction metrics are:

| Metric | Output |
|---|---:|
| Premium to unaffected price | 33.9% |
| Equity purchase price | $13,457.9 million |
| Transaction enterprise value | $12,702.9 million |
| EV / FY2026E Revenue | 11.1x |
| EV / FY2026E EBITDA | 38.4x |
| FY2026E P / E | 53.5x |

The offer sits within the trading P / E range and below the median precedent-premium indication, but it is materially above the fundamental EV / Revenue and EV / EBITDA ranges. Rockwell would therefore need substantial strategic value and credible synergies to defend the price.

## Avoiding double-counting

The premium-paid method already reflects control and some anticipated strategic value. It should not be combined mechanically with a standalone valuation and then increased again for all synergies. Synergies will be modeled separately to determine how much of the purchase premium Rockwell could economically support.

## Next analytical question

The next phase will test the $80 midpoint rather than treating it as final. The analysis will establish consideration mix, target cash treatment, financing assumptions, transaction fees, sources and uses, and Rockwell's pro forma leverage before the price is carried into purchase accounting and accretion/dilution.
