## Finalists and preliminary transaction rationales

### Finalist 1 — Rockwell Automation acquisition of Cognex

**Preliminary recommendation:** Preferred transaction for the project.

**Strategic rationale:** Rockwell provides industrial automation hardware, control systems, software, and lifecycle services used to operate manufacturing facilities. Cognex provides machine-vision systems that allow production equipment to inspect products, identify defects, guide robots, and read industrial codes. Acquiring Cognex would allow Rockwell to add a differentiated visual-data and inspection layer to its automation architecture, broaden its exposure to secular factory-automation investment, and offer customers a more integrated hardware, software, sensing, and analytics solution.

**Potential value-creation sources:**

- Cross-sell Cognex products through Rockwell's installed base and distribution relationships.
- Integrate machine-vision data into Rockwell control and manufacturing software.
- Expand Cognex in industries and geographies where Rockwell has deeper customer access.
- Reduce overlapping public-company, administrative, procurement, and go-to-market costs.
- Use Rockwell's scale to accelerate Cognex product development and commercialization.

**Principal risks:** Cognex's premium technology profile could require Rockwell to pay a substantial valuation multiple and control premium. Machine-vision demand is cyclical and exposed to automotive, electronics, logistics, and capital-spending conditions. Revenue synergies could also take longer to realize than cost synergies, and financing a material purchase price could pressure Rockwell's leverage or require stock consideration.

**Why it works as a portfolio project:** Both companies have substantial public histories and understandable financial statements. The deal supports a conventional DCF, trading comps, precedents, sources and uses, purchase accounting, accretion/dilution, and cost/revenue synergy analysis without forcing the core valuation to depend on an unprofitable target.

### Finalist 2 — Cisco acquisition of SentinelOne

**Preliminary recommendation:** Best technology and cybersecurity alternative.

**Strategic rationale:** Cisco is positioning security as a core component of its networking, observability, and AI infrastructure platform. SentinelOne would add cloud-native endpoint protection, extended detection and response, identity security, and autonomous threat detection. Combining SentinelOne with Cisco's security portfolio and Splunk's security analytics could give customers a more integrated platform spanning network, endpoint, identity, telemetry, and security operations.

**Potential value-creation sources:**

- Sell SentinelOne products through Cisco's global enterprise channel and installed base.
- Combine SentinelOne endpoint telemetry with Splunk analytics and Cisco network data.
- Improve customer retention through an integrated security platform.
- Reduce duplicated sales, marketing, public-company, and infrastructure costs.
- Accelerate SentinelOne's path to sustainable profitability using Cisco's operating scale.

**Principal risks:** Product overlap and platform integration could create customer confusion or disrupt SentinelOne's growth. The target's GAAP losses and significant stock-based compensation complicate valuation and accretion/dilution. Cisco could also pay too much for anticipated growth, while retaining SentinelOne's technical talent would be critical.

**Why it works as a portfolio project:** The transaction would demonstrate recurring-revenue analysis, ARR and RPO metrics, stock-based compensation adjustments, revenue-based valuation, cybersecurity comps, purchase accounting, and the financial effect of acquiring a high-growth target. It is more difficult and more assumption-sensitive than Rockwell/Cognex.

### Finalist 3 — Stryker acquisition of iRhythm

**Preliminary recommendation:** Best medtech and growth-investing alternative.

**Strategic rationale:** Stryker has expanded its cardiovascular portfolio, while iRhythm provides wearable cardiac-monitoring devices, diagnostic services, cloud analytics, and proprietary algorithms. Acquiring iRhythm could extend Stryker into ambulatory cardiac diagnostics, add a recurring data-and-service component, and create a broader cardiovascular platform spanning diagnosis, clinical decision support, and treatment-related products.

**Potential value-creation sources:**

- Expand iRhythm through Stryker's hospital and physician relationships.
- Accelerate international commercialization using Stryker's global infrastructure.
- Combine diagnostic insights with a broader cardiovascular product portfolio.
- Reduce overlapping corporate and public-company costs.
- Improve purchasing, logistics, and commercial efficiency through greater scale.

**Principal risks:** iRhythm depends on reimbursement, clinical adoption, regulatory approvals, and reliable service delivery. Stryker would need to prove that cardiac diagnostics are sufficiently connected to its existing portfolio. The target's recent profitability inflection makes forecast assumptions important, and Stryker's integration of other acquisitions could constrain capacity.

**Why it works as a portfolio project:** The deal combines a high-growth operating forecast with medtech comps and precedents, reimbursement and regulatory analysis, purchase accounting for developed technology and customer-related intangibles, and meaningful synergy and accretion/dilution sensitivities. It is moderately harder than Rockwell/Cognex but cleaner than Cisco/SentinelOne.

## Recommended selection

**Rockwell Automation / Cognex is the recommended transaction.** It offers the best balance of transaction realism, clean public data, forecastability, conventional valuation methods, meaningful financing analysis, credible synergies, and interview differentiation. Cisco/SentinelOne is the more attention-grabbing technology case, but its target profitability and stock-compensation issues would make the first full M&A model less stable. Stryker/iRhythm is compelling, but reimbursement and clinical-market assumptions add sector-specific complexity that may distract from learning the core M&A mechanics.

## Final selection

**Selected transaction:** Rockwell Automation, Inc. (NYSE: ROK) acquisition of Cognex Corporation (NASDAQ: CGNX).

**Selection date:** August 20, 2026.

**Selection rationale:** The pairing offers a logical industrial-automation thesis, profitable public companies with extensive SEC disclosure, a meaningful but analyzable transaction size, credible cost and revenue synergies, and enough complexity to demonstrate valuation, financing, purchase accounting, and accretion/dilution skills. It avoids making the first full M&A model dependent on an unprofitable target or highly specialized sector assumptions.

**Preliminary transaction form:** Negotiated acquisition of 100% of Cognex's fully diluted equity, with Cognex becoming a wholly owned Rockwell subsidiary.

**Preliminary consideration approach:** Mixed cash and Rockwell stock consideration. The cash portion may be funded with available cash and new debt; the stock portion would reduce incremental leverage but create share-count dilution. No percentage mix, offer price, premium, or financing amount is assumed at this stage. Those inputs will be derived later from valuation, financing-capacity, and accretion/dilution analyses.
