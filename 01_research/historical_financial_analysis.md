# Historical Financial Analysis

**Companies:** Cognex Corporation and Rockwell Automation  
**Period analyzed:** FY2021-FY2025  
**Units:** USD millions, except per-share data  
**Purpose:** Establish the historical operating, cash-flow, balance-sheet, and transaction-capacity foundation for the forecast and valuation.

## Methodology

Historical financial statements were spread from audited Forms 10-K and reconciled using SEC XBRL Company Facts. Reported line items remain distinct from calculated metrics. The workbook includes gross-profit, diluted-EPS, and balance-sheet tie-outs, plus a central checks sheet.

Rockwell's total segment operating earnings is a company-defined non-GAAP measure. It is used because it provides a consistent view of the buyer's underlying segment profitability across the period. It is not directly comparable to Cognex's GAAP operating income. Rockwell's reported net income and EPS also include non-operating items, including changes in the fair value of its PTC investment.

## Cognex historical performance

| Metric | FY2021 | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $1,037.1 | $1,006.1 | $837.5 | $914.5 | $994.4 |
| Revenue growth | — | (3.0%) | (16.8%) | 9.2% | 8.7% |
| Gross margin | 73.3% | 71.8% | 71.8% | 68.4% | 66.9% |
| Operating margin | 30.4% | 24.5% | 15.6% | 12.6% | 16.3% |
| Free cash flow | $298.6 | $223.7 | $89.8 | $134.0 | $236.8 |
| Free cash flow margin | 28.8% | 22.2% | 10.7% | 14.7% | 23.8% |
| Net cash and investments | $907.4 | $854.3 | $576.3 | $586.9 | $642.3 |

### Interpretation

Cognex combines high gross margins and low capital intensity with meaningful end-market cyclicality. Revenue contracted sharply in FY2023 before recovering in FY2024 and FY2025. Operating margin fell more severely than revenue, showing that the business has substantial operating leverage and maintains meaningful research and commercial investment through weaker periods.

Gross margin declined across the period, which will require explicit forecast assumptions rather than a simple return to the FY2021 peak. FY2025 free cash flow recovered strongly and exceeded net income, although one year of high conversion should not be extrapolated without examining working-capital timing and noncash expenses.

Cognex's $642 million of FY2025 cash and investments and absence of funded debt are important transaction considerations. These assets increase equity value but reduce net purchase cost when moving from equity value to enterprise value.

## Rockwell historical performance and transaction capacity

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| Revenue growth | — | 10.9% | 16.7% | (8.8%) | 0.9% |
| Total segment operating margin | 19.9% | 19.9% | 21.3% | 19.3% | 20.4% |
| Free cash flow | $1,140.7 | $682.0 | $1,213.5 | $639.0 | $1,358.0 |
| Free cash flow margin | 16.3% | 8.8% | 13.4% | 7.7% | 16.3% |
| Net debt | $2,808.6 | $2,973.6 | $1,798.9 | $2,396.7 | $2,148.0 |

### Interpretation

Rockwell has a substantially larger revenue and cash-flow base than Cognex, making the transaction strategically and financially plausible. Segment operating margin remained near 20% across the cycle even as revenue moved sharply, supporting the quality of the buyer's earnings base.

Free cash flow has been volatile, so financing capacity should not be sized from FY2025 alone. Rockwell ended FY2025 with approximately $2.1 billion of net debt. Its cash generation could support acquisition financing and post-close deleveraging, but the eventual sources-and-uses analysis must test interest expense, ratings considerations, leverage, and EPS dilution under several financing mixes.

## Forecast implications

1. Cognex revenue should be forecast through a cycle rather than by extrapolating FY2025 or the latest quarter.
2. Gross margin requires a defensible recovery or stabilization path because the FY2021-FY2025 trend was negative.
3. Research and development should remain a visible driver; cutting it mechanically could overstate sustainable margins.
4. Operating expenses should reflect operating leverage, but not an immediate return to the FY2021 margin peak.
5. Working capital and free-cash-flow conversion should be normalized using multiple years.
6. Rockwell financing assumptions should reflect normalized cash generation and existing net debt, not only a strong FY2025 result.
