# Project Mandate

## Hypothetical Rockwell Automation Acquisition of Cognex

**Project type:** Public-company strategic M&A valuation and transaction analysis  
**Status:** Hypothetical; neither company is represented as having proposed or endorsed this transaction  
**Analysis date:** August 20, 2026

## Transaction objective

Evaluate the strategic and financial merits of a hypothetical acquisition of Cognex Corporation (NASDAQ: CGNX) by Rockwell Automation, Inc. (NYSE: ROK). The analysis will determine a defensible standalone valuation range for Cognex, test a realistic purchase price and financing structure, quantify potential synergies, and assess the transaction's effect on Rockwell's earnings, capital structure, and shareholder value.

## Preliminary strategic thesis

Rockwell provides industrial automation hardware, control systems, software, and lifecycle services. Cognex provides machine-vision products and software that enable automated equipment to inspect products, identify defects, guide robots, and read industrial codes. Cognex could add a differentiated visual-data and inspection layer to Rockwell's automation architecture, allowing the combined company to offer a more integrated solution across factory control, sensing, machine vision, software, and analytics.

The preliminary value-creation thesis rests on four areas:

1. Cross-selling Cognex products through Rockwell's installed base and distribution relationships.
2. Integrating machine-vision data with Rockwell's control and manufacturing software.
3. Expanding Cognex into additional industries and geographies using Rockwell's commercial infrastructure.
4. Capturing corporate, go-to-market, procurement, and other operating efficiencies.

These benefits are hypotheses to be tested, not assumed facts. Revenue synergies will receive greater scrutiny than cost synergies because they depend on customer adoption, sales execution, pricing, competitive reactions, and realization timing.

## Preliminary transaction structure

- **Buyer:** Rockwell Automation, Inc.
- **Target:** Cognex Corporation
- **Form:** Negotiated acquisition of 100% of Cognex's fully diluted equity
- **Post-close ownership:** Cognex becomes a wholly owned Rockwell subsidiary
- **Consideration:** Preliminary mixed cash and Rockwell stock structure
- **Cash funding:** Potential combination of available cash and new debt
- **Stock funding:** New Rockwell shares issued to Cognex shareholders
- **Offer price and premium:** To be established after standalone valuation
- **Consideration mix:** To be optimized after financing-capacity and accretion/dilution analysis

No offer price, premium, synergy amount, financing amount, or consideration percentage is assumed in Step 1.

## Required analyses

The project will include historical financial analysis, a standalone operating forecast, DCF valuation, trading comparable companies, precedent transactions, a valuation summary and football field, purchase-price analysis, sources and uses, financing analysis, purchase accounting, accretion/dilution, synergy analysis, sensitivities, strategic rationale, transaction risks, and investment considerations.

## Key questions to answer

1. What is Cognex worth on a standalone basis?
2. What acquisition premium could Rockwell justify without transferring excessive value to Cognex shareholders?
3. How should Rockwell balance cash, new debt, and stock consideration?
4. What cost and revenue synergies are operationally credible, and when could they be realized?
5. Would the transaction be accretive or dilutive to Rockwell's EPS, and what assumptions drive the answer?
6. How would the acquisition affect Rockwell's leverage, liquidity, and financial flexibility?
7. What industrial-cycle, integration, customer, technology, regulatory, and execution risks could undermine the thesis?

## Initial risks

- Rockwell may need to pay a premium valuation for Cognex's technology and growth profile.
- Industrial automation and machine-vision demand can be affected by customer capital-spending cycles.
- Revenue synergies may be delayed or fail to materialize.
- Product, software, channel, and organizational integration may disrupt the businesses.
- New debt could pressure Rockwell's leverage and financial flexibility.
- Stock consideration could dilute Rockwell shareholders and transfer combined-company upside to Cognex shareholders.

## Deliverables

- Auditable Excel M&A valuation and transaction model
- Investment-banking-style pitchbook PDF
- Sourced research and methodology documentation
- Recruiter-friendly public GitHub repository
- Resume, LinkedIn, and interview-preparation materials

## Analytical standard

All material historical data will be traceable to primary sources where available. Forecasts and transaction inputs will be assumption-driven and clearly distinguished from reported information. The project will not present hypothetical terms or synergies as company guidance or established fact.
