# Operating Forecast

**Target:** Cognex Corporation  
**Buyer:** Rockwell Automation  
**Forecast period:** FY2026E-FY2030E  
**Selected case:** Base  
**Units:** USD millions, except per-share data

## Forecast methodology

The Cognex forecast is driver-based rather than a straight-line extrapolation. FY2026E is anchored to the midpoint of management's latest full-year guidance. FY2027E-FY2030E use visible analyst assumptions for revenue growth, gross margin, R&D, SG&A, D&A, tax rate, net interest and other income, capital expenditures, operating working capital, and diluted shares.

Base, upside, and downside assumptions are stored on the model's `Control` sheet. Changing the selected case updates the active drivers, income statement, earnings, and unlevered free cash flow without overwriting formulas.

The Rockwell forecast is intentionally narrower. It contains the revenue, enterprise operating margin, adjusted EPS, shares, adjusted net income, and free-cash-flow metrics required for later financing and accretion/dilution analysis. FY2026E is anchored to management guidance; outer years are analyst assumptions.

## Interpretation

The base case assumes FY2026 is a strong recovery year supported by current guidance, not a permanent growth rate. Revenue growth therefore steps down toward 6.5% by FY2030. This avoids valuing Cognex as though a cyclical rebound continues indefinitely.

Margins recover faster than revenue because Cognex has meaningful operating leverage: fixed and semi-fixed R&D, commercial, and infrastructure costs absorb more efficiently as volume returns. The model still keeps R&D near 13% of revenue and does not return operating margin immediately to the FY2021 peak.

Free cash flow is built from NOPAT, D&A, capital expenditures, and changes in operating working capital. Capital expenditures are normalized above the unusually low FY2025 level. Stock-based compensation is not added back to UFCF, avoiding an aggressive cash-flow benefit unless dilution is separately addressed in valuation.

## Key risks to the forecast

- Customer capital spending and order timing can make machine-vision demand volatile.
- The margin recovery depends on volume, product mix, pricing, and fixed-cost absorption.
- R&D underinvestment could weaken product leadership even if it increases near-term profit.
- Working-capital efficiency may not improve as assumed.
- FY2026 guidance includes near-term information that may not represent through-cycle performance.
