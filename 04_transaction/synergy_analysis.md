# Synergy Analysis

## Purpose

Synergy analysis estimates the incremental financial benefits Rockwell could realize by owning Cognex. The analysis is built independently from the merger model's breakeven requirement: the assumptions are based on identifiable cost pools and commercial drivers, and the resulting synergies are then compared with the amount required to eliminate EPS dilution.

This prevents the model from becoming circular. A breakeven synergy is an output that shows the burden created by the purchase price; it is not evidence that management can deliver that amount.

## Base-case cost synergies

| Category | FY2027E cost base | Savings assumption | Full run-rate synergy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Corporate G&A and back office | $379.0 million | 10.0% | $37.9 million |
| Sales and channel overlap | 379.0 | 7.5% | 28.4 |
| R&D and product roadmap | 166.5 | 3.0% | 5.0 |
| Procurement and cost of goods sold | 364.1 | 2.0% | 7.3 |
| Public-company costs | N/A | Fixed estimate | 12.0 |
| IT, facilities, and other | N/A | Fixed estimate | 8.0 |
| **Total cost synergy run-rate** |  |  | **$98.6 million** |

The largest opportunities are corporate-function consolidation and selective sales-channel rationalization. R&D savings are intentionally limited because aggressive product cuts could damage the machine-vision capabilities that create the strategic rationale for the acquisition.

Cost synergies phase in at 25%, 60%, 90%, and 100% from FY2027E through FY2030E. This recognizes that organizational changes, systems consolidation, and procurement initiatives take time to implement.

## Base-case revenue synergies

Revenue synergies are modeled separately from cost savings because they are less controllable. Incremental revenue equals 1.0%, 4.0%, 5.5%, and 7.5% of Cognex revenue from FY2027E through FY2030E. A 40.0% contribution margin converts incremental revenue into operating profit after allowing for product delivery, support, and selling costs.

| Revenue-synergy output | FY2027E | FY2028E | FY2029E | FY2030E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Incremental revenue | $12.4 million | $53.7 million | $79.0 million | $114.7 million |
| Operating-profit contribution | 5.0 | 21.5 | 31.6 | 45.9 |

The commercial thesis is that Rockwell's installed base and distribution reach could expand adoption of Cognex's machine-vision and identification products. Realization depends on sales-force incentives, product interoperability, customer acceptance, and the absence of channel conflict; therefore, these benefits carry more execution risk than cost savings.

## Integration costs

One-time integration costs are assumed at 1.5x the $98.6 million cost-synergy run-rate, or $147.9 million in total. The costs are incurred 50%, 35%, and 15% over FY2027E through FY2029E.

Integration costs are included in the reported-style EPS case because they are real cash and accounting expenses during implementation. They are excluded from recurring adjusted EPS once the integration period ends because they are not an ongoing cost of the combined company.

## EPS impact and breakeven comparison

| Result | FY2027E | FY2028E | FY2029E | FY2030E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Gross recurring pre-tax synergies | $29.6 million | $80.6 million | $120.3 million | $144.5 million |
| Net pre-tax synergy after integration costs | (44.3) | 28.9 | 98.1 | 144.5 |
| EPS accretion / (dilution), including PPA and integration costs | (22.6%) | (32.6%) | (25.3%) | (20.9%) |
| Adjusted EPS accretion / (dilution), excluding PPA and integration costs | (5.3%) | (7.8%) | (5.3%) | (3.7%) |
| Adjusted breakeven pre-tax synergy required | $144.1 million | $273.5 million | $256.3 million | $245.2 million |
| Breakeven synergy coverage | 20.6% | 29.5% | 46.9% | 58.9% |

The base case improves adjusted EPS relative to the no-synergy transaction, but it does not eliminate dilution. By FY2030E, recurring synergies cover only 58.9% of the $245.2 million pre-tax synergy required for adjusted EPS breakeven.

## Conclusion

At the illustrative $80 offer price, independently underwritten synergies do not support EPS neutrality. Rockwell would need a lower purchase price, a more favorable financing mix or cost, materially greater operating benefits, or a willingness to accept dilution in exchange for longer-term strategic value.

This conclusion should not be changed by simply assuming the breakeven synergy amount. Doing so would derive the operating plan from the desired EPS result and obscure overpayment and execution risk.
