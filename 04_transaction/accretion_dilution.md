# Accretion / Dilution Analysis

## Purpose

Accretion/dilution tests whether Rockwell's earnings per share would increase or decrease after acquiring Cognex. The analysis combines the buyer and target earnings forecasts, acquisition financing costs, purchase-accounting expense, and new shares issued.

The central formula is:

\[
\text{Pro Forma EPS}
=
\frac{\text{Buyer Net Income} + \text{Target Contribution} - \text{After-Tax Transaction Costs}}{\text{Buyer Shares} + \text{Weighted New Shares}}
\]

\[
\text{Accretion / Dilution}
=
\frac{\text{Pro Forma EPS}}{\text{Buyer Standalone EPS}}-1
\]

Buyer standalone earnings are based on Rockwell's adjusted EPS guidance and the model's forecast growth assumptions. Accordingly, the output is a merger-model EPS comparison rather than a fully audited GAAP forecast.

## Base-case conventions

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---:|
| Illustrative closing date | March 31, 2027 |
| FY2027 ownership factor | 50.0% |
| Buyer tax rate | 21.0% |
| New debt | $4,000.0 million |
| New debt interest rate | 6.0% |
| Foregone cash yield | 4.0% |
| Debt-fee amortization period | 5 years |
| New Rockwell shares issued | 21.835 million |

The pre-synergy analysis excludes transaction benefits to isolate the burden created by purchase price and financing. The separately completed synergy analysis then adds independently underwritten cost and revenue benefits. One-time transaction fees are excluded from recurring EPS, although their first-year effect is presented separately.

## Pro forma earnings bridge

| FY2027E item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Rockwell standalone adjusted net income | $1,551.4 million |
| Acquired Cognex net income contribution | 139.7 million |
| After-tax financing costs | ($101.3 million) |
| After-tax purchase-accounting expense | ($235.5 million) |
| Pro forma net income including PPA | **$1,354.2 million** |
| Pro forma net income excluding PPA | **$1,589.7 million** |

Only half of Cognex's FY2027E earnings, acquisition financing costs, purchase-accounting expense, and newly issued shares are included in FY2027E because the assumed transaction closes halfway through Rockwell's fiscal year.

## EPS results

| Result | FY2027E | FY2028E | FY2029E | FY2030E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Rockwell standalone adjusted EPS | $13.94 | $14.78 | $15.51 | $16.29 |
| Pro forma EPS including PPA | $11.08 | $9.78 | $10.99 | $12.01 |
| Accretion / (dilution) including PPA | **(20.5%)** | **(33.8%)** | **(29.1%)** | **(26.3%)** |
| Pro forma EPS excluding PPA | $13.01 | $13.14 | $13.97 | $14.81 |
| Adjusted accretion / (dilution) excluding PPA | **(6.7%)** | **(11.1%)** | **(9.9%)** | **(9.1%)** |

The transaction remains dilutive after excluding purchase-accounting expense. This indicates that dilution is not merely an accounting issue: Cognex's standalone earnings contribution is insufficient to offset the incremental financing costs and 21.835 million new Rockwell shares at the proposed purchase price.

One-time transaction fees reduce illustrative FY2027E EPS further to approximately $9.98, but they are excluded from headline recurring accretion/dilution because they do not represent an ongoing earnings burden.

## Breakeven synergies

The breakeven calculation determines the pre-tax cost-synergy run-rate required to make pro forma EPS equal Rockwell's standalone EPS. It is a threshold, not a forecast.

| Required annualized pre-tax synergies | FY2027E | FY2028E | FY2029E | FY2030E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Including purchase accounting | $884.5 million | $836.1 million | $751.7 million | $706.9 million |
| Excluding purchase accounting | $288.1 million | $273.5 million | $256.3 million | $245.2 million |

The FY2027E amount is annualized from six months of transaction ownership. The adjusted FY2027E threshold equals approximately 23.2% of Cognex revenue and 78.1% of Cognex EBITDA. That is a demanding burden and indicates that economically credible synergy underwriting will be central to determining whether the $80 offer is supportable.

## Interpretation

- Stock consideration protects Rockwell's balance sheet but increases the pro forma share count and creates dilution.
- Debt financing limits share issuance but produces a material after-tax interest burden.
- Purchase-accounting amortization makes reported dilution substantially worse but is primarily non-cash.
- The persistence of adjusted dilution suggests the acquisition price is high relative to Cognex's forecast earnings.
- An accretive transaction is not automatically value-creating, and a dilutive transaction is not automatically value-destroying; strategic value, cash flow, risk, and the price paid must also be considered.

The bottom-up synergy case is built independently from the breakeven requirement rather than inserting the breakeven amount as an assumed benefit.
