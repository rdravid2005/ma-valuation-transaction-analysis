# Purchase Accounting

## Purpose

Purchase accounting estimates how Rockwell would record Cognex's acquired assets and liabilities at fair value. It converts the acquisition price into identifiable net assets, deferred taxes, and residual goodwill, then calculates the incremental non-cash expense required for accretion/dilution analysis.

This is an illustrative merger-model allocation. An actual transaction would require specialist valuation work, legal-entity tax analysis, and management estimates at closing.

## Starting balance-sheet values

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Purchase consideration | $13,457.9 million |
| Cognex shareholders' equity | $1,629.6 million |
| Less: existing goodwill | ($381.4 million) |
| Less: existing intangible assets | ($64.5 million) |
| Tangible book value | **$1,183.7 million** |

Cognex's July 5, 2026 unaudited balance sheet provides the latest book equity, goodwill, and intangible-asset balances. Historical target goodwill is removed because the acquisition establishes a new goodwill balance based on Rockwell's consideration and the fair value of the acquired net assets.

## Preliminary fair-value allocation

| Asset | % of consideration | Fair value | Useful life |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Customer relationships | 22.0% | $2,960.7 million | 15 years |
| Developed technology | 12.0% | $1,615.0 million | 7 years |
| Trade names | 3.0% | $403.7 million | 15 years |
| Order backlog | 1.0% | $134.6 million | 2 years |
| Inventory step-up | 0.5% | $67.3 million | 1 year |
| PP&E step-up | 0.5% | $67.3 million | 10 years |
| **Total identifiable write-ups** | **39.0%** | **$5,248.6 million** | — |

The allocation emphasizes customer relationships and developed technology because Cognex's strategic value is primarily associated with its installed base, machine-vision software, algorithms, product portfolio, and technical intellectual property. These percentages and useful lives are analyst assumptions, not company guidance.

## Purchase-price allocation bridge

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Purchase consideration | $13,457.9 million |
| Tangible book value | $1,183.7 million |
| Gross fair-value write-ups | 5,248.6 million |
| Less: incremental deferred tax liability | ($1,102.2 million) |
| Net identifiable assets | **$5,330.1 million** |
| New goodwill | **$8,127.8 million** |
| Goodwill as a percentage of consideration | **60.4%** |

The model assumes the tax basis of the written-up assets does not increase. The difference between their financial-reporting basis and tax basis creates an initial deferred tax liability. Because that liability reduces identifiable net assets, it increases acquisition goodwill.

Goodwill is the residual amount that cannot be assigned to separately identifiable assets. It can represent expected synergies, assembled workforce, future products, market access, and strategic value, but it also exposes Rockwell to impairment risk if the transaction underperforms.

## Incremental expense

| Expense | Annualized run rate |
|---|---:|
| Intangible-asset amortization | $522.3 million |
| Incremental PP&E depreciation | 6.7 million |
| Inventory step-up expense | 67.3 million |
| Total annualized PPA expense | **$596.3 million** |

The illustrative March 31, 2027 close creates six months of ownership in Rockwell's fiscal year ending September 30, 2027. The resulting first-year purchase-accounting expense is:

| FY2027E impact | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Pre-tax PPA expense | $298.2 million |
| Deferred tax benefit | ($62.6 million) |
| After-tax PPA expense | **$235.5 million** |

The expense is non-cash, but it reduces reported GAAP earnings and therefore matters for GAAP accretion/dilution. The next step will separately show reported EPS and an adjusted view excluding acquisition-related amortization so the economic and accounting effects are not confused.

## Key risks and limitations

- The allocation is highly sensitive to the fair values and useful lives assigned to customer and technology assets.
- More value assigned to finite-lived intangibles generally means greater amortization and more reported EPS dilution.
- More residual goodwill reduces annual amortization but increases impairment exposure.
- Inventory step-up is temporary and primarily affects early post-close earnings.
- Actual tax treatment could differ materially by jurisdiction and transaction structure.
