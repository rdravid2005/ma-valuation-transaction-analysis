# Purchase Price, Sources & Uses, and Financing

## Purpose

This analysis translates the selected offer price into the amount paid for Cognex's equity and operating assets, then tests how Rockwell could fund the transaction without exceeding an illustrative leverage ceiling. All figures are hypothetical and shown in USD millions except per-share data and multiples.

## Key transaction assumptions

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---:|
| Offer price per Cognex share | $80.00 |
| Unaffected Cognex share price | $59.73 |
| Premium to unaffected price | 33.9% |
| Cognex diluted shares outstanding | 168.224 |
| Cash / stock consideration | 30% / 70% |
| Transaction fee | 1.0% of equity purchase price |
| New-debt financing fee | 1.0% of new debt |
| New debt interest rate | 6.0% |
| Illustrative closing date | March 31, 2027 |

The $80.00 offer sits above Cognex's unaffected trading price and within the observed premium range, but it also implies demanding operating multiples. It is therefore a proposed negotiation price rather than a claim that Rockwell should pay any price to obtain control.

## Financing rationale

The proposed acquisition is large relative to Rockwell's existing liquidity and balance sheet. A 70% stock component limits incremental debt and cash usage, although it creates dilution and gives Cognex shareholders participation in the combined company. The 30% cash component provides meaningful cash value to target shareholders while avoiding an all-debt structure.

The model assumes $4.0 billion of new debt, split evenly between a term loan or bridge facility and senior notes. This is an illustrative permanent-financing framework, not committed financing. Rockwell contributes the remaining $212.0 million from balance-sheet cash, leaving approximately $211.0 million of standalone buyer cash before adding Cognex's acquired cash.

Gross leverage remains slightly below the model's illustrative 3.0x ceiling without credit for transaction synergies. That treatment is deliberately conservative: projected synergies may support financing discussions, but they should not be required for the initial leverage case to work.

Rockwell's March 2026 Form 10-Q reported $423 million of cash and $3.691 billion of total debt and disclosed a $1.5 billion revolving facility plus a minimum 3.0x EBITDA-to-interest covenant. The model uses the filing to anchor existing capital structure and covenant context, but the proposed debt mix, rate, fees, leverage ceiling, and funding proportions remain hypothetical assumptions.

## Limitations and next analysis

- Share prices are fixed as of the analysis date; an actual stock transaction would require an exchange-ratio structure and collar analysis.
- The model assumes no debt repayment, divestitures, or committed acquisition facility beyond the stated sources.
- Purchase accounting, incremental amortization, foregone interest income, and transaction-related tax effects are not yet reflected.
- Cost and revenue synergies are excluded from the base leverage metrics and will be evaluated separately.

The next step is purchase accounting, which will estimate goodwill, intangible-asset write-ups, deferred taxes, and incremental amortization before those effects flow into accretion/dilution.
