# Transaction Sensitivity Analysis

## Purpose

Sensitivity analysis tests whether the transaction conclusion changes when uncertain assumptions move. Unlike a valuation range pasted into a presentation, each sensitivity cell recalculates the relevant purchase-price, financing, share-count, and earnings mechanics.

The primary output is FY2030E adjusted EPS accretion or dilution after recurring synergies. Purchase-accounting amortization and one-time integration costs are excluded so the tables isolate the longer-term operating and financing economics.

## Offer price and synergy realization

The first table varies the offer price from $76 to $84 per Cognex share and applies 50% to 150% of the independently underwritten FY2030E recurring synergies.

| Offer price | 50% synergies | 75% | 100% | 125% | 150% |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $76 | (5.3%) | (4.0%) | (2.6%) | (1.3%) | **0.1%** |
| $78 | (5.9%) | (4.5%) | (3.2%) | (1.8%) | (0.5%) |
| $80 | (6.4%) | (5.1%) | **(3.7%)** | (2.4%) | (1.1%) |
| $82 | (7.0%) | (5.6%) | (4.3%) | (3.0%) | (1.6%) |
| $84 | (7.5%) | (6.2%) | (4.9%) | (3.5%) | (2.2%) |

The base case is $80 per share with 100% of modeled synergies. Only the most favorable tested combination—$76 per share and 150% synergy realization—becomes marginally accretive.

The table demonstrates that purchase price is the most direct negotiable lever. A lower price reduces the stock issued, Rockwell cash required, and the earnings burden that synergies must overcome. Higher synergies help, but using an upside synergy case to justify the offer transfers execution risk to Rockwell shareholders.

## Consideration mix and debt rate

The second table varies stock consideration from 50% to 90% and the acquisition-debt interest rate from 5.0% to 7.0%. Rockwell cash used remains fixed at the base level, and debt becomes the balancing source.

| Stock consideration | 5.0% rate | 5.5% | 6.0% | 6.5% | 7.0% |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50% | (2.9%) | (4.2%) | (5.5%) | (6.8%) | (8.1%) |
| 60% | (2.6%) | (3.6%) | (4.6%) | (5.6%) | (6.6%) |
| 70% | (2.3%) | (3.0%) | **(3.7%)** | (4.5%) | (5.2%) |
| 80% | (2.0%) | (2.5%) | (2.9%) | (3.4%) | (3.9%) |
| 90% | (1.7%) | (1.9%) | (2.2%) | (2.4%) | (2.6%) |

More stock reduces acquisition debt and interest expense but creates additional shares. More cash or debt reduces share issuance but increases the financing burden and balance-sheet risk. Within the tested range, financing optimization improves the result but does not produce accretion.

## Conclusion

The base recommendation is not highly sensitive to a single small assumption change. The transaction generally remains dilutive across plausible offer-price, synergy, consideration, and debt-rate scenarios.

Rockwell should therefore proceed only if:

- The purchase price can be reduced materially;
- Commercial and operational diligence supports benefits beyond the base case;
- Management accepts near-term dilution in exchange for defensible long-term strategic value; and
- The integration and financing risks remain within the company's risk tolerance.
