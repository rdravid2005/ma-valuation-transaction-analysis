# Transaction Risks and Diligence Priorities

## Risk summary

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Why it matters | Potential mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Valuation and overpayment | High | High | The $80 offer exceeds most standalone valuation indications and requires substantial strategic value. | Negotiate a lower price, use disciplined walk-away thresholds, and avoid using upside synergies as the base case. |
| Persistent EPS dilution | High | High | The transaction remains dilutive after excluding PPA and including modeled recurring synergies. | Reprice the transaction, optimize financing, stage consideration, or accept dilution only with a strong long-term value case. |
| Revenue-synergy realization | Medium-high | High | Cross-selling depends on sales incentives, product compatibility, channel acceptance, and customer behavior. | Develop account-level revenue plans, assign owners, risk-adjust benefits, and track customer retention and pipeline conversion. |
| Cost-synergy execution | Medium | Medium-high | Savings require organizational changes and may disrupt operations or customer coverage. | Use function-level workplans, protect customer-facing capacity, and monitor savings net of stranded costs. |
| Technology and product integration | Medium | High | Poor integration could weaken Cognex's innovation pace or create an unclear combined product architecture. | Preserve critical engineering teams, establish product-roadmap governance, and limit early R&D reductions. |
| Talent and cultural retention | Medium-high | High | Cognex's value depends on specialized engineering, software, and commercial talent. | Use retention packages, clear decision rights, and a deliberate operating-model integration plan. |
| Customer and channel conflict | Medium | High | Overlapping sales coverage or platform preferences could create customer uncertainty and channel disruption. | Communicate product positioning early and use customer-level transition plans. |
| Industrial-cycle exposure | Medium | Medium-high | Cognex demand is exposed to factory automation spending, electronics, logistics, and capital-investment cycles. | Use downside forecasts, maintain liquidity, and avoid underwriting peak-cycle growth. |
| Financing and credit-rating pressure | Medium | High | Additional debt and transaction cash usage reduce financial flexibility and may affect borrowing costs. | Preserve minimum liquidity, stage refinancing, maintain leverage limits, and consider greater stock consideration. |
| Purchase-accounting and goodwill impairment | Medium | Medium-high | Approximately $8.1 billion of new goodwill creates impairment risk if expected cash flows underperform. | Use conservative cash-flow forecasts, monitor reporting-unit performance, and avoid treating goodwill as an operating asset. |
| Regulatory and transaction timing | Low-medium | Medium | Review delays could increase financing costs, distract management, or delay synergy realization. | Build realistic closing assumptions, maintain financing flexibility, and prepare regulatory workstreams early. |
| Integration-cost overruns | Medium | Medium | Systems, restructuring, retention, and advisory costs could exceed the modeled $147.9 million. | Establish a detailed integration budget, contingency reserve, approval controls, and monthly reporting. |

## Priority diligence questions

### Commercial

1. Which Rockwell customer accounts have a documented need for Cognex products but do not currently purchase them?
2. How much of the revenue-synergy pipeline is supported by named customers rather than broad market assumptions?
3. Could Rockwell's channel relationships conflict with Cognex's existing distributors or direct-sales model?
4. What customer attrition or delayed purchasing could result from the transaction announcement?

### Products and technology

1. Which products are genuinely complementary, and which overlap with existing Rockwell capabilities?
2. What engineering talent, source code, patents, or product roadmaps are essential to preserve?
3. What investment is required to integrate Cognex technology with Rockwell's software and automation architecture?
4. Would product integration create cybersecurity, data-governance, or interoperability risks?

### Cost structure and integration

1. Which specific positions, facilities, systems, and third-party contracts support each cost-synergy category?
2. Which costs are removable, and which would remain as stranded costs?
3. What severance, retention, systems, and facility expenses are required to achieve the savings?
4. Who owns each synergy initiative, and what are the 30-, 100-, and 365-day milestones?

### Financial and accounting

1. How sensitive are Cognex's margins and working capital to an industrial downturn?
2. What purchase-price allocation would be supported by an independent valuation specialist?
3. How much of the new goodwill would be assigned to each reporting unit, and what cash flows support it?
4. Could tax treatment, transaction-fee deductibility, or debt-financing terms differ materially from the illustrative assumptions?

### Deal structure

1. What is Rockwell's maximum acceptable offer price under base-case synergies?
2. Could an earnout, contingent value right, or staged investment reduce upfront overpayment risk?
3. What financing mix preserves investment-grade credit metrics and adequate post-close liquidity?
4. What conditions would cause Rockwell to renegotiate or walk away?

## Overall risk assessment

The strategic rationale is understandable, but the financial case is demanding. The most important risks are overpayment, persistent EPS dilution, and reliance on revenue synergies that are less controllable than cost savings. These risks do not automatically make the transaction unattractive, but they raise the standard of commercial, technology, and integration evidence required before recommending the acquisition.
